About me

Connecting material design
with biological function.

I am a biomedical engineer working at the intersection of nanomaterials, RNA delivery, and disease-relevant tissue models.

At UConn, I work with Professor Yupeng Chen to understand how a carrier’s molecular architecture shapes what it can deliver—and where. My research combines material assembly and characterization with cellular imaging and biological evaluation, connecting the properties of a nanoparticle to the function of its therapeutic cargo.

My path spans biosensor research at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology, a master’s degree in Bioengineering at UC San Diego, and a Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering at UConn. That experience has shaped a central question in my work: how can we design materials around the biological barriers a therapy must cross?

The research question

A therapy is only as effective
as the journey it can make.

Reaching a tissue, entering a cell, and releasing cargo are distinct challenges. My research brings materials engineering to each step, with published studies in RNA delivery and preclinical cancer models.

  1. 01

    Reach the tissue

    Study how carrier assembly and geometry influence transport through dense biological environments.

  2. 02

    Release the cargo

    Investigate endosomal escape so RNA can reach its intracellular site of action.

  3. 03

    Measure the function

    Connect delivery with gene silencing and therapeutic response in relevant experimental models.

Looking ahead

From delivery mechanisms
to targeted RNA medicine.

My long-term vision is to develop RNA nanomedicines for inflammatory and degenerative diseases. I aim to connect molecular design, biological transport, and disease-relevant testing in a research program that explains both how delivery works and why it matters.
