My research asks how a nanomaterial’s molecular building blocks, assembly, and geometry affect transport across biological barriers. I work with DNA-inspired Janus base nanomaterials and combine formulation, characterization, and biological experiments to study therapeutic delivery.

<div class="research-direction" markdown="1">
<span class="direction-number">01</span>

## RNA delivery & endosomal escape

Getting RNA into a cell is only part of the delivery problem. It also needs to reach the intracellular compartment where it can act. My work investigates Janus base nanopieces as carriers for RNA and examines how material properties influence endosomal escape and functional delivery.

My first-author [PNAS study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2104511118) examined DNA-inspired nanomaterials for enhanced endosomal escape. More recently, I contributed as co-corresponding author to a [Materials Today Advances study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mtadv.2026.100845) on doxorubicin and siRNA co-delivery in preclinical cancer models.

<p class="research-keywords">Janus base nanopieces / siRNA / mRNA / intracellular transport</p>
</div>

## Nanoparticle design & tissue transport

The structure of a delivery vehicle can influence how it moves through tissue. I combine computation-informed design with experimental formulation and characterization to study rod-shaped nanoparticles and their interactions with biological environments.

As co-first author of our [Journal of Controlled Release study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2025.114169), I contributed to the design and evaluation of rod-shaped carriers for tumoral targeting. This work motivates further investigation of relationships between nanoparticle assembly, physicochemical properties, and delivery performance.

<p class="research-keywords">Molecular assembly / rod-shaped carriers / tumor penetration / structure–function relationships</p>
</div>

## RNA therapeutics & engineered tissue models

I am developing Janus base nanopieces for Atsttrin mRNA delivery and a cartilage-on-a-chip platform to investigate osteoarthritis. This ongoing work connects therapeutic formulation with human tissue models, with the aim of studying cartilage degradation and treatment response.

The project was selected for the [2026–2027 ORS/Bethel Fellowship](https://www.bethel.pitt.edu/2026/09/03/advancing-discovery-bmrc-welcomes-its-newest-cohort-of-bethel-fellows/). Current development includes a fabricated platform prototype and preliminary molecular-response studies; therapeutic performance remains under investigation.

<p class="research-keywords">Osteoarthritis / Atsttrin mRNA / cartilage-on-a-chip / preclinical models</p>
</div>

## Broader collaborations

I also contribute to collaborative research on Janus base nanomaterials in microgravity. UConn’s work with Eascra Biotechnologies and Axiom Space brings materials research to the International Space Station. [Read UConn’s overview of the collaboration](https://today.uconn.edu/2023/06/axiom-space-astronauts-perform-life-saving-nanomaterial-experiments-for-uconns-biomedical-engineering-researchers/).
